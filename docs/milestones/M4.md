# M4: Economy

M4 puts a price on the city. Roads cost money to build and to keep, buildings pay tax, developers pay a charge for each new building, and every dollar lands in one treasury that closes its budget once a game month. Running out of money stops building, and the city can borrow. Clearing an occupied building means paying its owner and moving its people. The first population milestones pay grants and unlock densities 2 and 3 and City Hall, which gives the budget a place on the map.

The full context is in [the PRD](../PRD.md), under "M4 Economy" and its sections "Eminent domain" and "Prices in 2026 dollars", "Removing things" under M2, "Progression" and "Landmarks" under "Land value and progression", "Satisfaction" under M8, and "Sound".

## Exit test

All of these pass in one Playwright walkthrough, on WebGPU where available and on the WebGL 2 fallback.

1. Start a new city. The top bar shows the treasury at the starting grant.
2. Draw a street. The road tool shows its price before the click. After it, the treasury has dropped by exactly that price, the activity log names the player and the amount, and the "money spent" sound plays.
3. Zone R1, C1, O1 and I1 along streets joined to an outside connection. Buildings grow, and each new one adds its development charge to the treasury.
4. Run a game month at 4x. The budget closes. The budget panel shows last month's income by tax and by development charges, each on its own line, and its expenses by road upkeep. Income less expenses equals the change in the treasury over the month.
5. Raise the residential tax rate. The residential bar's tooltip shows tax pulling it down, and the next month's residential tax income is higher. Set it to zero and the bar rises.
6. Raise the commercial development charge to its top. Commercial building slows. Set it to zero and the charge line for commercial shows nothing the next month.
7. Spend the treasury down to the overdraft. The road tool shows the price in red and the reason, a click is refused with the "rejected" sound, and upkeep keeps running.
8. Take a loan. The treasury rises by the principal, the budget panel lists the loan with its monthly repayment, and the next month's close charges it. The credit limit is shown, and it is larger once the city has grown.
9. Grow the city past the first milestone. A grant is paid, the "milestone reached" sound plays, and City Hall and density 2 unlock. Before that the zone tool shows density 2 locked, with the population that unlocks it.
10. Place City Hall on a road. Its price is charged, its cells cannot be zoned, and its upkeep joins the budget. Clicking it opens the budget panel.
11. Point the bulldozer at an occupied house. The tag gives the compensation: the building's value, the relocation payment for its residents, and the premium. Click. The treasury drops by exactly that, and the displaced pops' satisfaction drops. Point it at a vacant building. The tag gives the demolition cost only.
12. Bulldoze a stretch of street with buildings on it. The tag gives the road's demolition less its refund, plus the compensation for every building it takes, and the treasury drops by that total.
13. Turn on the budget overlay. A compact block of density 2 shows a net income above zero, and a long cul-de-sac of houses shows one below it.
14. Reload the page. The treasury, the month's ledger, the history, the rates, the loans, the milestones reached and City Hall come back.
15. A replay of the recorded command log from the same seed produces the same state hash.

Two more are scenario tests in Vitest, because they take game years. A compact grid of density 1 houses with shops and work roughly breaks even, and the same population spread along long cul-de-sacs loses money. A new city started on its grant can build a first town that breaks even before density 2 unlocks.

## Numbers fixed for M4

| What | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Money | Whole in-game dollars in a float64, as rule 9 says. 2026 prices, each with its source year, raised by the escalation factor of 1.1085 a year in `roads.json` |
| Treasury | One per city. A new city starts with the starting grant. It may fall to a small overdraft and no further |
| Budget | Closes on the first tick of each game month. Income and expenses are kept by line for the month in progress and the month before. The treasury, income, expenses and tax income are sampled into the statistics history each month |
| Taxes | Residential: a yearly rate on property value. Commercial: a rate on sales. Office: a rate on output. Industrial: a rate on output. Mixed: property tax on its homes and sales tax on its shops |
| Tax sliders | Four, for residential, commercial, office and industrial. Mixed pays the residential and commercial rates. Each rate is a part of its zone's demand bar |
| Development charges | A lump sum per new private building, scaled by its size. Redevelopment pays only on the size it adds. One slider per zone type. A higher rate slows building in that zone |
| Density ratios to aim for | Tax per cell about 1x at density 1, 10x at density 2 and 50x at density 3. Net after roads about zero at density 1 |
| Construction | Instant. The full price is charged on the tick the command applies |
| Upkeep | A yearly share of build cost, per road type, charged a day at a time |
| Eminent domain | Occupied: building value, plus a relocation payment per resident or job displaced, plus a premium. Vacant or abandoned: demolition only. City-owned: demolition, less a refund share of the build cost. Empty cells: free |
| Loans | Two or three open at once. Each has a principal, a rate and a term, and repays monthly. The credit limit grows with population and last year's tax income |
| Milestones | About ten population milestones up to 500,000. Each pays a one-time grant. M4's unlocks are density 2, density 3, City Hall and larger loans |
| City Hall | One per city. Unlocked by the first milestone. Placed by the player on a large lot with road frontage. City-owned. Grows at three milestones after it is placed |
| Game time | 100 ticks to a game day and a game month of about five minutes at 1x, from M0, to be tuned in issue 19 now M4 is playable |
| Scale checkpoints | The generated cities of 50,000, 200,000 and 500,000 residents from M3, measured at Low and Balanced |

Every rate, price, share and threshold goes in `packages/sim/balance/`, in `economy.json` from issue 01 and `progression.json` from issue 12, with a source year on every price. Issue 19 does the tuning pass. They are starting values to tune.

## Decisions to make inside M4

- **What a building is worth.** Property tax and the eminent domain price both need a building's value in dollars. Issue 01 proposes land value times the building's floor space, where floor space follows its room or its jobs, with dollars per square metre by zone in `economy.json`. Redevelopment already has a worth of its own (M3-14: land value times lot cells times 1, 3 and 8 by density), and those weights do not give the 1x, 10x and 50x tax per cell the PRD asks for. Issue 01 measures both on the scale cities and proposes whether redevelopment moves onto the dollar value.
- **When money moves.** The budget closes monthly, but a treasury that jumps once every five minutes hides cause and effect. Issue 02 proposes that taxes and upkeep move the treasury a day at a time and the close only files the month into history. A yearly amount is spread over the days exactly, with no fraction of a dollar stored: day d of a year charges floor(yearly × (d + 1) / 365) less floor(yearly × d / 365).
- **Spending that has no building yet.** At honest prices a city of 500,000 takes in near $3B a year, most of it spent on services that arrive in M8. With only roads to pay for, an M4 city would be rich and the economy dull. The proposal is an "other city spending" line per resident, priced from the Toronto and Chicago budgets in the PRD, that M8 and M9 replace a service at a time. The alternative is to set taxes to what M4 costs and raise them as each service lands, which breaks every balance figure once per module. **Settled when issue 02 started: the per-resident line.** Taxes stay at honest rates, issue 02 adds the line to the ledger at $0, and issue 19 prices it.
- **What the overdraft blocks.** The PRD says building and zoning commands fail past the overdraft. Zoning is free, and a broke city recovers by growing. Issue 03 proposes that only commands that cost money are refused, and zoning stays open. If agreed, the PRD changes in that pull request. **Settled in issue 03: only commands that cost money are refused.** The PRD's overdraft paragraph says so.
- **Undo and money.** Issue 03 proposes that undoing a road command reverses exactly the money that command moved, so an undone road is refunded in full and an undone bulldoze takes back its refund. The amount goes on the undo record. Upkeep paid in between stays paid. Compensation paid for buildings stays paid too, since undo never brings buildings back. **Done in issue 03.**
- **What commercial sales and industrial output follow.** The PRD ties sales to the customers a shop serves and industrial output to the goods it ships, and both arrive with trips and freight in M5. Issue 06 proposes that in M4 a shop's sales follow its jobs filled, scaled down when the city has more shop jobs than its residents' spending power supports, and that industrial and office output follow jobs filled, industrial only while its road reaches an outside connection. M5 swaps in trips and freight.
- **How a development charge slows building.** Issue 08 proposes that the rate scales the grower's daily quota in that zone before it is rounded, with the fraction left over built on that share of days from the seeded generator, since a quota rounded up never falls below one a day. It does not enter the demand bar, so a high charge slows construction without telling newcomers the city is less attractive. **Done in issue 08, with a floor:** the want is at least one a day while the bar is above zero, before the charge's share, so with no charge a positive bar still builds one a day and at the top of the range a one-a-day zone builds on about four days in ten. Without it mixed ground starved at low commercial demand (the M3 walkthrough). The chance is drawn on the day's last tick, only for a zone with a free cell, so a city with nothing to build never moves the generator. Mixed has its own rate and ledger line, so there are five charge sliders and four tax sliders.
- **Old cities and the locks.** An M3 city may already be zoned at densities 2 and 3 with no milestones recorded. Issue 12 proposes that a city loaded from an older save is credited with every milestone its highest population passed, without their grants, and gets the starting grant once. A lock stops new painting at that density and never stops growth on cells already zoned.
- **How long an expropriation is remembered.** Issue 09 proposes a list in state of the expropriations of the last game year, each with its place and the number of people displaced, and issue 10 derives the neighbours' satisfaction hit from that list on each pop's daily visit. The displaced pops carry their own hit wherever they moved, as the PRD says, through a mark on the pop row. Redevelopment counts too, at a smaller weight issue 10 picks. Whether redevelopment's displacement counts, at a smaller weight, is issue 10's call. **Done in issue 09:** each record has the tick, the lot's centre, the people and jobs displaced and the player; records a game year old are dropped, and the list is capped by `expropriationRecords.max` in `economy.json`. **Done in issue 10:** neighbours within 300 m lose 0.2 points per person displaced, at most 20, and a pop the city moved loses 10 plus 0.01 per person its home held, at most 20, both fading over a game year. Redevelopment counts at half the moved pop's own hit and not for its neighbours, since it leaves no expropriation record. The PRD's "Eminent domain" has the rest.
- **City Hall's lot.** Issue 13 proposes a fixed footprint in cells on one side of a road, placed on free cells only. Its cells leave the zoning and cannot be painted while it stands. Placing it over buildings is refused rather than expropriating them.
- **How a road's upkeep splits between blocks.** The budget overlay needs each block's share of the roads it uses. Issue 18 proposes half of each segment to each block either side, and all of it to the one block a cul-de-sac sits in. **Done in issue 18:** the odd dollar goes to the block on the segment's left, a segment with a block on one side only gives it all to that block, and buildings and roads on open ground count toward their 500 m chunk. A building counts toward the block its lot fronts.
- **Loan arithmetic.** Interest and repayments must come out the same on every host. Issue 11 proposes a fixed monthly repayment worked out by repeated multiplication, no `Math.pow`, with interest each month the floor of the balance times the monthly rate, and the last payment taking whatever remains.
- **The day length.** The PRD says to tune it once M4 is playable. Issue 19 plays budget cycles at 100 ticks a day and says whether it stays.

## Out of scope

Service buildings and their budget sliders, which are M8. Utilities and their plants, which are M9. Transit fares and operating costs, which are M6 and M7. Rail upkeep, which arrives with track in M7.

Traffic in the economy. Congestion lowering business tax, shopping trips behind commercial sales and freight behind industrial output are M5. Road types beyond the three M1 built, so no highway prices in play.

The standing cost-sharing programs for rail, freeways and hospitals that later milestones open. M4 builds the milestone list and pays one-time grants. Each later module registers its own unlocks and programs.

Construction that takes time and is paid in stages, which is v2. Council votes, which are after v1.

The satisfaction overlay, the click-through on a pop's score, and the approval rating in the top bar, which are M8. M4 adds two inputs to the score, the tax on a pop's home and recent displacement, and keeps the record of each expropriation for the overlay to read later.

Landmarks other than City Hall. The central station is M7 and the rest are M11.

Per-player spending limits and roles. In M4 every command still comes from the one local player, and the log names who spent what so M10 has it.

Per-citizen agents, in any form. Money is counted per building and per pop.

## Risks to settle early

The tax sweep is the first. A tax is a function of a building's value, its people and its jobs, and the 500,000 resident city has about 50,000 buildings. Issue 05 visits each building once a game day, a stride of them per tick as redevelopment does, and charges its day's tax on that visit. Anything read city-wide, such as the month's income by line or the tax base, is a running sum kept as buildings come and go, not a pass. The grower's cold pass in #366 is the reason: a city-wide pass once a day was the worst tick of M3.

Exact money is the second. The treasury is in the state hash, so every charge has to come out the same on every host, a server included. Daily shares, interest and compensation use whole dollars and only the operations rule 6 allows. Issue 02 adds a test helper that checks the treasury against the ledger at every step of a long session.

The third is balance. Honest prices and honest taxes do not agree with a game that has only roads to spend on, as the decision above says. Issue 19 adds the money to `pnpm balance`, and the scenario tests in the exit test pin the density ratios. The "other city spending" question is settled: a per-resident line, priced in issue 19.

The M3 generators are the fourth. `generateScaleCity`, the standard balance cities, the replay fixtures and the M3 walkthroughs build tens of thousands of segments with ordinary commands and zone densities 2 and 3 at once. Past the starting grant and before the milestones they would be refused. Issues 03 and 12 add test helpers that fund a city and credit its milestones, and each checks the scale cities still meet their targets.

The sim chain is 01, 02, 05, 06 and 07: value, the treasury, then the taxes and what they do to demand. After the treasury it fans out. Construction costs in 03, upkeep in 04, development charges in 08 and milestones in 12 each touch a different line of the ledger and can go side by side. Eminent domain in 09 builds on 03's charging and 01's value. The client work starts at 14, once there is money to show, and follows whichever sim issue feeds it.

Three things to watch when working in parallel. Issues 02, 03, 07, 08, 09, 10, 11, 12 and 13 each bump `SNAPSHOT_VERSION`, so a branch takes the next free number when it rebases. The protocol version goes up with 02, 07, 08, 11, 12 and 13. The treasury is in the state hash from issue 02, so from issue 03 on every replay fixture with a road in it changes, again with 04, again with 05 and 06 for any fixture with buildings, and with 08. Expect to refresh fixture hashes after a rebase and say why in the pull request.
