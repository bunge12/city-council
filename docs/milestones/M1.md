# M1: Road networks

M1 is the first milestone a player can do something in. They draw roads on the M0 map, the roads join into a network, and the sim can route across it. No traffic, zoning or money yet.

The full context is in [the PRD](../PRD.md), under "M1 Road networks". The three things rail needs from M1 are under "M7 Rail transport".

## Exit test

All of these pass in one Playwright walkthrough, on WebGPU where available and on the WebGL 2 fallback.

1. Draw a straight street. It appears on the map and in the activity log with a player id and a tick.
2. Draw a second street across the first. The first splits and a four-way intersection forms.
3. Draw a curved avenue whose end snaps to an existing node, and a road that snaps to 90 degrees off an existing segment.
4. Draw a road across water. The part over water becomes a bridge. A road up a slope too steep for its type is refused, and the preview says why.
5. The preview shows the length and the cost before the click. Nothing is charged, because the treasury arrives in M4.
6. Upgrade a street to an avenue in place. Bulldoze a segment. Undo both, one step at a time.
7. Pick two points on the network. The route between them is highlighted. Bulldoze the only link between them and the pathfinder reports no route.
8. Every outside connection at the map edge is reachable from a road joined to it.
9. Reload the page. The same network comes back.
10. A replay of the recorded command log from the same seed produces the same state hash.
11. Close a loop of streets. The debug panel outlines the enclosed block and shows its area. Bulldoze one side and the block goes.
12. Draw a street beside an existing one. It snaps parallel at twelve cells, and its end lines up with a node two blocks away.
13. Drag out a grid from an existing street and confirm. The streets are built and joined in one log entry, they stop at water, and one undo removes them all.

## Numbers fixed for M1

| What | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Buildable road types | Street with 2 lanes, avenue with 4, one-way with 2 |
| Highway | In the data and at the outside connections. Not buildable until M5 |
| Network types | Road, rail and path are stored. Only road is buildable |
| Segment shape | A straight line, or one quadratic curve with a single control point |
| Vertical level | Stored at each end of every segment: ground, bridge, tunnel and two elevated levels. Players build ground and bridge only. Segments at different levels with 6.5 m between them cross without an intersection (issue 22) |
| Routing graph | One edge per direction per segment. A one-way has one edge |
| Outside connections | Two or three, fixed at the map edge, placed from the seed |
| Spatial index cell | One 500 m chunk, the same grid the renderer uses |
| Guides | Angle snap, guide lines, parallel offset, length snap and a readout. All client work, each with a toggle |
| Grid | One `BuildRoadGrid` command per grid. Straight streets only, cut at water and steep ground, capped in `roads.json` |
| Blocks | Faces of the graph of ground-level road segments. Bridges, tunnels, rail and paths do not bound one |
| Scale check | A generated network of 30,000 segments |

Speeds, capacities, widths, slope limits, curve limits and prices are set in `packages/sim/balance/roads.json` by issue 01. They are starting values to tune.

## Decisions to make inside M1

- **How a road meets the ground.** There is no terraforming, so a road follows the terrain. Issue 03 samples the ground along the path and refuses a road whose grade passes the limit for its type. If draped roads look wrong on rolling ground, the fallback is a straight grade between node heights with short embankments drawn by the client. Decided in issue 03: roads drape, with node heights from the terrain and the grade checked between ground samples at most 8 m apart. See "How a road meets the ground" in the PRD.
- **What "bridges over water above a height" means.** I read the PRD line as this: where a road crosses water, that span becomes a bridge with its deck a set clearance above the water. Bridging a dry valley is left for M5, with elevated roads. Issue 11 records this in the PRD. Say so if you meant something else. Decided in issue 11: the deck sits 5 m above the water, with a ramp at the type's slope limit from a node on each bank, and the sim moves each bank node back from the water until its ramp fits. See "Bridges" in the PRD.
- **Where derived data lives.** The spatial index and the routing graph are rebuilt from the graph arrays. They are not saved. Issue 02 picks one home for both, and tests that a city saved and reloaded mid-run behaves the same as one that never stopped. The M0 review asks for exactly this check. Decided in issue 02: a cache keyed by the state object, rebuilt on first use, outside `SimState`. See rule 7 under "Rules the sim follows" in the PRD.
- **The price of a local street.** The PRD lists it as still to price. Issue 01 looks for a source. If it finds none, the number goes in marked as a placeholder and stays on the PRD list.
- **What a failed undo does.** Issue 14 decides whether an undo that is no longer valid stays on the player's stack or is dropped. Decided in issue 14: it stays at the top, since a refused command changes nothing. The player clears what is in the way and tries again. See "Undo" in the PRD.

## Out of scope

Zoning cells, buildings, pops, money changing hands, traffic volumes, overlays, highways and ramps as buildable roads, tunnels and elevated roads as things a player can build, rail and paths as buildable networks, transit lines, bike lanes, multiplayer.

Snapping to zoning cells is in the PRD list for M1, but the cells do not exist until M2. It moves to M2.

Junction types with delays, such as signals and roundabouts, belong to M5. So do the control to raise and lower a road, ramps, tunnels and the underground view. M1 only stores levels and applies the crossing rule (see "Levels and grade separation" in the PRD).

The grid tool's preview shows the eminent domain cost of buildings in the way, in the PRD. M1 has no buildings and no money, so that part waits for M2 and M4.

M1 finds blocks but nothing uses them yet. Whole-block parks are M8 (see "Parks and recreation" in the PRD). Through-lots and per-block overlays come with the modules that need them.

## One risk to settle early

The PRD names the road tool as the hardest client work in the project. So the order below gets a playable slice first: straight roads, intersections, rendering and a basic tool by issue 07. Curves, snapping and bridges come after that. The sim graph never depends on the mesh, so the visuals can improve without a new save version.

### What can run in parallel

The numbering is one safe order, not the only one. Each GitHub issue lists what blocks it and what it blocks. An issue can start as soon as everything that blocks it is merged.

| 1 | 01 | M0 |
| 2 | 02 | 01 |
| 3 | 03 | 02 |

| 6 | 07, 09 and 15 | 06 |
| 7 | 08, 11, 13, 16 and 18 | 07 for 08. 09 for 11. 07 and 12 for 13. 12 and 15 for 16. 09 and 12 for 18 |
| 8 | 10, 14, 19 and 22 | 08 and 09 for 10. 12 and 13 for 14. 08 for 19. 11 for 22 |
| 9 | 20 | 14 |
| 10 | 21 | 08 and 20 |
| 11 | 17 | everything |

There are three chains after issue 06. The drawing chain is 07, 08 and 10, with the guides in 19 hanging off 08. The shape chain is 09, 11 and 22. Blocks, 18, needs both the shape and editing chains to have started, at 09 and 12. The editing chain is 12, 13 and 14. Routing, 15 and 16, hangs off 04 and 06 and needs 12 only for the last step. The grid, 20 and 21, comes last: its command waits for undo in 14 and its tool for snapping in 08.

Two things to watch when working in parallel. Issues 14 and 16 both bump `SNAPSHOT_VERSION`, so whichever merges second takes the next number and rebases its migration. Issue 22 bumps it too if it changes how a level is stored. Issue 18 adds a derived structure like the spatial index and needs no snapshot bump. Issue 20 needs none: the undo record from 14 is a journal of every graph change one command made, so it holds a whole grid under one log entry. Issues 09, 11, 12 and 22 all change `BuildRoad` validation and the replay fixtures, so expect to refresh fixture hashes after a rebase and say why in the pull request.

## When M1 closes

M1 closed with issue 17 (#43). The exit test runs as `e2e/m1-walkthrough.spec.ts`, and the measurements on the 30,000 segment city are under "M1 exit numbers" in the PRD.

Update the PRD with anything that changed and tick the M1 row in the roadmap. Then run the two-tab spike the PRD describes: host the sim in a Node process, connect two tabs and draw roads together. Two things the M0 review left for the spike: the server gives each connection its own player id and stamps it on that connection's commands, as `SimHost` already does for its one connection, and commands get a payload size cap (see the open questions in the PRD). After that, write `M2.md` and its issues.
