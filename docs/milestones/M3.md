# M3: Demand

M3 puts people in the buildings. Pops move in, take jobs and leave when there are none, and four demand bars decide what grows. It is where the city starts to answer the player: zone only homes and it stalls, add work and it picks up. It also brings the first land value, the population count with its history, and the first sound. No money yet.

The full context is in [the PRD](../PRD.md), under "M3 Demand" and its sections, "Jobs per building" and "Rezoning" under M2, "Land value", "Satisfaction" and "Sound".

## Exit test

All of these pass in one Playwright walkthrough, on WebGPU where available and on the WebGL 2 fallback.

1. Start from a city with a street joined to an outside connection and zone it R1. The residential bar is above zero. Houses grow, and the top bar counts population up from zero with its arrow pointing up.
2. Keep the city all residential. Unemployment rises, the residential bar falls below zero, building stops and some pops leave. The bar's tooltip says why.
3. Zone commercial and industrial ground. Shops and factories grow, pops take the jobs, and the residential bar comes back above zero.
4. Zone office ground. Small offices grow from the start.
5. Zone mixed ground. It builds only while the residential and commercial bars are both above zero.
6. Zone an empty R3 lot of 4 × 4 or more. A tower grows there as soon as residential demand is above zero, with no density 1 or 2 building first.
7. Zone two strips, one by the water and one beside factories. The land value overlay shows the first as worth more, and it fills first.
8. Point at a house. The readout gives the people living there against its room. Point at a shop. It gives jobs filled against jobs offered.
9. Bulldoze the workplaces. Pops lose their jobs, then leave. Homes stand vacant, then abandoned, and both look it. The population falls and the arrow points down.
10. Bulldoze an occupied house while other homes have room. Its pops move into them and the population does not drop.
11. Rezone built R1 ground to R3 while residential demand is high. In time a house there is replaced by a tower, and its pops find other homes.
12. Run some game years at 4x. The statistics panel graphs population over time and lists births, deaths, arrivals and departures per year.
13. Place a road, paint a zone, bulldoze, and try something the sim rejects. Each makes its sound. Turn the interface volume to zero and they stop. The setting survives a reload.
14. Reload the page. The same pops, jobs, demand bars, population and history come back.
15. A replay of the recorded command log from the same seed produces the same state hash.

Two more are scenario tests in Vitest, because they take game decades. A city with no jobs loses population. A new street of houses fills with families, dips as children leave, and climbs back toward a steady level.

## Numbers fixed for M3

| What | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Pop | A block of 1 to about 100 people who share a home building, a workplace and a profile. A house is one pop. A tower is several |
| Pop fields | Home, workplace or none, size, education and wealth at three levels each, has children, satisfaction from 0 to 100 |
| Jobs per building | `max(1, round(jobsPerCell × width × depth))`, with the rates from "Jobs per building" in the PRD |
| Demand bars | Four: residential, commercial, office and industrial. Each from -100 to 100, recomputed once per game day |
| Mixed | No bar. It grows when residential and commercial are both above zero |
| Density | Whatever the cell is zoned for, as soon as demand is above zero. All three can still be painted. The unlock arrives in M4 |
| Job matching | A pop picks among the open jobs its education allows, weighted toward nearer ones by free-flow travel time |
| Satisfaction | One input in M3: having a job, and one that fits the pop's education |
| Turnover | Births, children leaving, deaths and household replacement, a little every game day from the seeded generator. About 18 game years for children to leave |
| Land value inputs | Road access, distance to water, distance to the city centre, pollution from industry |
| Statistics | Running totals in state. One sample of each per game month, 1,200 samples kept, which is a hundred game years |
| Pace | About 17,000 new residents per game year when demand is healthy, from "Pacing" in the PRD |
| Game time | 100 ticks to a game day, from M0. A game year is about an hour at 1x |
| Sound | In the client only, synthesised in code. Volumes for interface and ambience, and a mute |
| Scale checkpoints | Generated cities of 50,000, 200,000 and 500,000 residents, measured at Low and Balanced |

Every rate, weight and threshold goes in `packages/sim/balance/`, in `demand.json`, `pops.json` and `landvalue.json`, set by issues 01 and 13. Issue 21 does the first tuning pass. They are starting values to tune.

## Decisions to make inside M3

- **How a pop is cut from a building.** The PRD gives the size range and says a tower's pops are split by where they work. Issue 03 proposes one pop per household group up to a size cap set by the building's density, and issue 09 decides whether a pop that finds only part of the jobs it needs splits in two.
- **What the client holds.** A full city is 40,000 to 150,000 pops, and turnover touches many of them every day. Issue 05 proposes that deltas carry the totals, the history and each building's people and jobs filled, and that pop rows travel only in a snapshot. The client has no use for single pops until M5. Say so if the mirror should hold them all.
- **How a city starts.** With no residents and no jobs, every input to the bars is zero. Issue 06 proposes a small pull from outside that holds the residential bar above zero until the city has a few hundred residents, so the first houses always come.
- **Office standing in for industry.** The PRD's land check shows industry cannot keep a real share of jobs as a city densifies. Issue 06 proposes that industrial demand counts open office jobs against it, so a dense city leans on offices, and issue 21 tunes it.
- **What reaches the outside.** The PRD brings pops in through the outside connections. Issue 08 proposes that a home takes newcomers only if its road has a route to one. A street cut off from the map edge then builds but stays empty, and the readout says why.
- **How the grower paces itself.** M2 fills any zoning in a game month. Issue 07 proposes that each zone builds only while its bar is above zero, at a pace that follows the bar. Issue 08 stops homes while the city already has more than a set share of empty room, and issue 09 does the same for workplaces and open jobs.
- **Travel time at scale.** A route per pop per job is too slow at 500,000 residents. Issue 23 decides how coarse the travel times are. The proposal is a table of free-flow times between 500 m chunks, derived from the routing graph and rebuilt when roads change.
- **How long before a jobless pop leaves, and a vacant building is abandoned.** Issues 10 and 24 pick the numbers. The proposal is about one game month without work before satisfaction is low enough to leave, and about three game months vacant before a building is abandoned.
- **What happens to an abandoned building.** Issue 24 proposes that it stands, takes pops or jobs again when demand returns, and is cleared by the grower when its ground is zoned for something else. Issue 14 does the clearing.
- **Where the city centre is.** Land value needs one. Issue 13 proposes the middle of the city's jobs, recomputed monthly, and the middle of its homes while there are no jobs.
- **Where land value runs.** The PRD puts land value in a background worker. With four cheap inputs, issue 13 proposed to compute it in the step, a slice of the map per tick. Issue 13 decided it is derived instead: a value is worked out from state when it is asked for, and only the city centre is state, worked out once a game month in the step. See "Land value" in the PRD. If the 500,000 checkpoint says the centre is too slow for the step, it moves to a worker, and its result lands on a fixed tick, under rule 5 in CLAUDE.md.
- **Satisfaction and demand.** The PRD has average satisfaction feed residential demand. With one input in M3 it mostly repeats unemployment, which the bar already reads. Issue 10 adds it anyway with a small weight, so M8 only has to add inputs.
- **When an owner redevelops.** The PRD says when the new building would be worth clearly more than the old. Issue 14 turns that into a number from land value, the zoned density and demand.
- **The denser scale network.** The M1 scale city holds 57,345 buildings, and the town grown on it has room for about 475,000 residents only when every home is full. Issue 20 decides how the generator makes the three checkpoint cities, and whether 500,000 needs more frontage or more density 3.

## Out of scope

Money in any form: taxes, upkeep, construction cost, the treasury, the eminent domain price and the relocation payment, which are M4. The bulldozer's price tag stays at $0. The population milestones, the starting grant and the unlock for densities 2 and 3 are M4 too, with the "money spent" and "milestone reached" sounds.

Traffic. Commutes do not load onto roads, and there are no cars. Commute length is free-flow travel time until M5, and long commutes do not lower demand until then. Freight, shopping trips and the export input to industrial demand beyond "a route to an outside connection exists" are M5.

Residential tax, services, parks, pollution from plants, noise and utilities as inputs to demand, satisfaction or land value. Each arrives with its module. Schools raising the education of newcomers and clinics lowering deaths are M8.

The satisfaction overlay, the click-through that lists what moves a pop's score, and the approval rating in the top bar, which the PRD specifies under M8. M3 keeps the field and uses it to decide who leaves.

The satisfaction hit from displacement. M3 moves displaced pops and records nothing against the city. The hit and the record of each expropriation are M4.

Ambient sound other than wind and construction, and music. Recordings of any kind: M3's sound is all synthesised, so the open question on recordings does not block it.

Per-citizen agents, in any form. A pop is the smallest thing the sim counts.

The two-tab spike stays separate work, as in M2.

## Risks to settle early

The pop count is the first. A full city is up to 150,000 pops, and demand, job matching, satisfaction and turnover each visit them. None may scan every pop in one tick. Each daily system works through a slice of the pops per tick, so its cost is spread over the game day, and issue 03 sets the pattern the others follow. Issue 20 builds the 500,000 resident city before the last sim and client issues are finished, so the numbers come while there is time to act on them.

Job matching is the second. Its cost grows with pops times jobs. The answer in the PRD is the same as for traffic: work between zones, not between buildings. Issue 23 builds the travel times between zones and issue 09 measures matching on the scale town, before satisfaction and turnover are built on it.

The third is tuning. M3 is the first milestone that can fail by being dull or unstable with every test green: bars that swing, a city that never stalls or never recovers. Issue 21 adds the balance check script the PRD asks for, and the scenario tests in the sim issues pin the behaviours the exit test names.

The sim chain is 01, 03, 04, 06, 07, 08 and 09. After job matching it fans out: leaving in 10, turnover in 11 and displacement in 12 touch different parts of a pop's life and can go side by side. Three things run beside the chain from the start. Travel times in 23 need only the roads. Land value in 13 needs only the buildings and their jobs, and joins the chain at 25, where it starts to steer the grower. Sound, 02 and 19, touches no sim code. The client work, 15 to 18, needs the protocol from 05 and then follows whichever sim issue feeds it.

Three things to watch when working in parallel. Issues 03, 04, 06, 08, 10, 12, 14 and 24 each bump `SNAPSHOT_VERSION`, and 07, 09, 13, 23 and 25 do if they add state. Several of these can be open at once, 13 beside the whole chain and 10, 11 and 12 beside each other, so a branch takes the next free number when it rebases. Issues 05, 06, 10 and 24 raise the protocol version, and 09 and 13 do if the delta gains a field. From issue 07 on, the grower no longer builds unless demand asks, and from 25 it builds in a different order, so every replay fixture with zoning in it changes twice. Expect to refresh fixture hashes after a rebase and say why in the pull request.
